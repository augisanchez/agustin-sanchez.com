# The Photographic Journal (ThePhotographicJournal.com): A Comprehensive Overview

## TL;DR
- The Photographic Journal (TPJ) is an independent, Los Angeles–based online photography publication founded in 2012 by designer-photographer Agustin Sanchez, publishing in-depth interviews, photo essays, and features that focus on the human and creative process behind photography rather than gear or technique.
- It punches above its weight editorially: alongside emerging and amateur photographers who submit work through an always-open submissions process, it has run long-form interviews with major figures including David LaChapelle, Gregory Crewdson, Alec Soth, Duane Michals, Roger Ballen, Martin Schoeller, and the musician Moby.
- Run by founder/publisher Agustin Sanchez and longtime Editor-in-Chief Lou Noble, TPJ positions itself as a humanist, community-first alternative to gear-review sites and to the algorithmic churn of Instagram, and is regularly cited as a notable independent photography publication.

## Key Findings
- **What it is:** A digital ("online") journal that, in its own words, "publishes Interviews, Articles, Features, and Photo Essays about Photography and its creative process." It is distinct from — and frequently confused with — the Royal Photographic Society's much older print periodical also called *The Photographic Journal*. Wikipedia explicitly notes the RPS journal "is not affiliated with ThePhotographicJournal.com."
- **Founding:** Founded in 2012. The site's first interview ("Interview 001") was published October 25, 2012 — an interview of Lou Noble conducted by founder Agustin Sanchez, framed as "the beginning of our journey." (Some third-party sources, including Wikipedia and a Substack roundup, list 2013, but TPJ's own timestamped content supports 2012.)
- **Mission:** From a single original purpose — "To chat with the photographers we love, climb into their brains and figure out what makes them tick" — it expanded into "a platform where photographers of all levels can explore the medium they love," publishing "boundary-pushing images that make us feel more connected on a human level."
- **Editorial focus:** Humanist. Its LinkedIn description states it focuses on "the people behind the lens — an element more than a camera spec, or firmware update — the human element."
- **Content types:** Three main editorial buckets — Interviews, Photo Essays (Essays), and Features — organized further by category: Conceptual, Documentary, Environmental, Event, Fashion, Fine Art, Portrait, Reviews, and TPJ Spotlight.
- **Who runs it:** Agustin Sanchez (Founder, Publisher) and Lou Noble (Editor-in-Chief), supported by a small masthead.
- **Submissions:** Always open, photo-essay-focused, with permissive creative guidelines and a roughly 1–2 week reply window.
- **Reach/reputation:** 24K followers, 614 following, and 5,333 posts on Instagram (@tpj) per the account's own profile; brand partnerships including Polaroid, Lomography, Vans, and others; cited by third parties as a respected independent photography publication and "a haven for serious amateurs and emerging artists."

## Details

### What the publication is and how it's positioned
The Photographic Journal is an independent online magazine devoted to photography as a creative and human practice. Its tagline across the site is consistent: it "publishes Interviews, Articles, Features, and Photo Essays about Photography and its creative process." On LinkedIn the publication describes itself as "a digital journal focused on humanist concepts, capturing why our participants create while simultaneously highlighting their work," and says it "publishes in-depth interviews and photo essays weekly, working with talented and thoughtful artists. We hope to inspire and educate people on creative process and thought."

This humanist framing is the key to its positioning. Rather than competing with gear-review and technique sites, TPJ explicitly de-emphasizes "a camera spec, or firmware update" in favor of "the people behind the lens." A Substack roundup of independent photography publications captured its niche well, describing TPJ as "a haven for serious amateurs and emerging artists who just want a place for their work to be seen by those who care," and framing the broader genre as a corrective to Instagram ("It's time we look beyond Instagram"). An Arts University Bournemouth library guide lists TPJ among recommended photography journals, summarizing it as offering "Boundary-pushing images that connect on a human level helping the creative community better serve each other as mutual sources of inspiration."

A recurring point of confusion is the name. The older, unrelated *The Photographic Journal* is the periodical of the Royal Photographic Society, which "was first published on 3 March 1853 and it has been published continuously ever since" (per Wikipedia), making it the oldest photographic periodical in the world (now the *RPS Journal*). Wikipedia's article on that publication adds a disambiguating note that it "is not affiliated with ThePhotographicJournal.com, which is an online publication." The two share a name but nothing else.

### History and founding
TPJ was founded in 2012. The clearest primary evidence is the site's own first published interview — labeled "Interview 001," dated October 25, 2012 — which was an interview of Lou Noble conducted by founder Agustin Sanchez. The foreword to that piece reads: "We enjoyed our chat with him and are proud to invite you to the beginning of our journey." The About page reiterates the year: "When we founded The Photographic Journal in 2012 we had a single purpose: To chat with the photographers we love, climb into their brains and figure out what makes them tick."

Some secondary sources cite 2013 (Wikipedia says the site "began in 2013," and a Substack feature says it "started back in 2013"), but the publication's own timestamped content points to 2012 as both the founding and first-publication year. The discrepancy appears to be a third-party approximation rather than a genuine "conceived 2012 / launched 2013" distinction.

Agustin Sanchez is consistently credited as the sole founder ("Founder, Publisher"). Lou Noble was not a co-founder; he joined as a contributor and rose to Editor-in-Chief. In an interview with Flash Punk, Noble explained: "My working with TPJ is entirely due to the founder, Agustin Sanchez, asking me to come on board. First I was just doing the occasional interview, but I enjoyed it so much, I started taking on more responsibilities." Notably, the publication's very first published piece had Noble as the subject — interviewed by Sanchez — before Noble transitioned into interviewing others and eventually leading the editorial operation.

Over time, the publication broadened from its original interview-only ambition. The About page describes the evolution: "Over the years, we've expanded beyond that original aim, as we've come into contact with more and more talented people. Today we serve as a platform where photographers of all levels can explore the medium they love."

### Content: types, style, and approach
TPJ's content falls into three editorial formats, all browsable from the Articles hub:

1. **Interviews** — The publication's flagship format. The interviews section describes its mission: "Conversations with the photography heroes, peers, subjects and models who inspire and delight us. Our mission is to dive deep into an artist's process, to understand what drives them to create." These are long-form, conversational, and candid; each is typically prefaced with a personal foreword and noted as "edited for clarity and content." Photographer Chris Buck, a subject, remarked that Noble's interview of him "is unique for its level of candor… I say things here that I usually keep to myself."

2. **Photo Essays** — Described as "Visual series that explore themes and moods, and take us into the eye of the beholder." This is the most voluminous and community-driven section, with hundreds of essays spanning documentary, portrait, conceptual, and personal work from contributors worldwide. Topics range widely — e.g., queer friendship ("On Friendship"), modern dating ("Guise"), wildfire documentary ("LA Fires," "Eaton Fire"), bullfighting, motorsport, and travel.

3. **Features** — Longer-form or more experimental written/visual pieces, including deeply personal first-person narrative essays (e.g., "Context," a wrenching account of pregnancy loss).

The house style is intimate, literary, and unpretentious — often first-person, frequently emotional, and consistently focused on motivation and meaning over technical craft. Editorial voice is warm and slightly irreverent (the submissions page invites photographers to "Send pix (via WeTransfer), let us kibbitz, make some magic").

### Submission process
Submissions are "ALWAYS OPEN," and the focus is on photo essays. Per the submissions page, the publication accepts "photo essays of all shapes and sizes," with replies expected "in 1-2 weeks." Submissions are sent via WeTransfer to submissions@thephotographicjournal.com. The guidelines:
- Any theme is welcome.
- A preference for work with "a strong POV, a good deal of energy in the subjects and settings," and photos that "strongly convey emotion and/or thematic subtext."
- A narrative or thematic connection across the images.
- Between 10–20 images.
- Sized 1800px–2400px wide at 72dpi.
- An intro of no more than 200 words (may be edited by staff).
- A title (may be edited by staff) and a profile pic.
- TPJ reserves the right to reject a photo essay any time before publication.
- The Contact page specifies that submitted work should be unpublished ("be it in print/online/social networks/website").

The tone is encouraging and low-barrier: "SEND US SOMETHING THAT EXPRESSES WHO YOU ARE AS LOUDLY AND WONDERFULLY AS POSSIBLE. WE LOVE YOU!" Beyond essays, TPJ also solicits Instagram takeover submissions. For brand and press inquiries, the Contact page lists separate addresses (opportunities@ for advertising/collaborations, press@ for journalists, info@ for general).

## Recommendations
- **For photographers seeking exposure:** TPJ is one of the more accessible high-quality outlets to pitch. Prepare a cohesive 10–20 image photo essay with a strong point of view and a tight sub-200-word intro, ensure the work is previously unpublished, and submit via WeTransfer. Expect a 1–2 week turnaround. This is a strong fit for emerging and serious-amateur photographers; it is less of a fit for pure gear/technique content.
- **For readers/researchers:** Use the Interviews section for long-form, candid conversations with both canonical and working photographers — it is the publication's strongest and most distinctive asset. Use the category filters (Documentary, Fine Art, Portrait, etc.) to navigate the large photo-essay archive.
- **For brands/partners:** TPJ actively seeks brand collaborations (opportunities@thephotographicjournal.com) and has a track record with camera/film brands (Polaroid, Lomography) and lifestyle brands (Vans); its humanist, creator-first audience suits authentic creative collaborations over hard-sell advertising.
- **Benchmarks that would change this assessment:** A significant decline in publishing cadence, loss of high-profile interview access, or closure of submissions would reduce its value as an exposure venue. Conversely, continued marquee interviews (like the 2026 LaChapelle piece) reinforce its standing.

## Caveats
- **Name confusion:** Do not conflate ThePhotographicJournal.com with the Royal Photographic Society's *The Photographic Journal* (1853–present). They are entirely unrelated despite the identical name.
- **Founding-date conflict:** TPJ's own content supports a 2012 founding (Interview 001 dated October 25, 2012), but some third-party sources state 2013. I judge 2012 the more accurate date based on primary evidence.
- **Some data is from aggregators:** Employee counts, revenue figures, and a "Founded 2011" date appear on third-party business-data sites (LinkedIn, RocketReach, ZoomInfo) and should be treated as unverified/approximate; the LinkedIn "2011" date conflicts with the site's own 2012 claim and is likely inaccurate.
- **Follower counts are point-in-time:** The 24K Instagram figure reflects a recent snapshot and will change.
- **Spam artifact:** The live site's article-index pages currently display some injected foreign-language spam text (Russian casino links) in the footer area, suggesting possible site security/maintenance issues at the time of review; this does not affect the editorial content itself.
